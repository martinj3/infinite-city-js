Plan: smarter intersection behavior for traffic   [IMPLEMENTED -- see below]
================================================
(Written by the model that built traffic.js's driving model, as a guide for a
follow-up implementation. Read the "Traffic" section of CLAUDE.md first; every
term here -- planExit, turnPlan, senseRoad, driveCar's speed section, aPlan,
stopDone, waitT -- is defined there or in traffic.js itself.)

This half is done. It was built as written -- one boxClear gate, pure reads, no
node-owned state, patience as the backstop -- and the "What NOT to build" list
held up completely. Five things came out differently, and they are in
"Implementation notes" at the end of this section. The lane-changing plan
further down is still unbuilt and still reads on its own.

The goal: a car at a stop sign or an uncontrolled intersection should wait for
conflicting traffic instead of driving through it. Four-way stops should take
turns. Left turns should yield to oncoming cars. Everything else about the
driving model already works and should not change.

The one new concept: "may I enter the box yet?"
-----------------------------------------------
All of this is a single new gate, not a new control loop. driveCar already has
exactly one place where a stopped car decides to go: waitT expires and sets
stopDone. Replace "dwell then go" with "dwell, then go only when clear", and add
the same clearness test for cars that never stop (uncontrolled crossings, and
the through street at a two-way). Concretely:

1. Write one function, `boxClear(c)`, that answers: will any conflicting car be
   in the intersection during my crossing window? Keep it a pure read -- no
   state on other cars, no reservations, no negotiation (see "what not to
   build" below).

2. Call it from two places in driveCar's speed section:
   - At a stop sign: when waitT expires, only set stopDone if boxClear(c);
     otherwise hold vT = 0 and re-test each frame (cheap; the car is stopped).
   - Unsigned approaches (no sign for my slot): if !boxClear(c) treat the node
     like a stop line -- reuse the existing required-deceleration branch with a
     goal of 0 at STOP_LINE_BACK. This gives yielding at uncontrolled
     crossings and two-way through streets for free, using code that already
     exists. Gate it on distance (only test within ~100ft of the node) so
     mid-block cars never pay for it.

How boxClear should judge conflicts
-----------------------------------
Work per approaching car o at my endNode (find them by iterating traffic once:
o.endNode === c.endNode, or o already in a turn whose plan came from this node
-- store node on the turnPlan when you build it). For each, estimate arrival
and clearing times from what is already known: distance to node = len - o.pos
(or arcRem mid-turn), speed = o.speed. My own crossing time = (distance through
box + my length) / max(my vTurn or approach speed, 5). Conflict if the time
windows overlap, with a safety margin that is a driver personality field
(aggressive drivers accept smaller gaps -- add `gapAccept`, ~1.5-3.5s, to
makeDriver).

Prune hard before doing any arithmetic:
- A car whose path cannot cross mine is not a conflict. Compute this from
  slots, not geometry: my entry slot and exit slot are knowable at planExit
  time (store them on turnPlan/next); same for others. A right turn conflicts
  only with traffic entering from my left and oncoming lefts; straight-through
  conflicts with cross traffic and oncoming lefts; a left conflicts with
  everything except a right from my right. Encode this as a small static table
  keyed by (myEntry->myExit, theirEntry->theirExit) relative slot pairs --
  fifteen-ish entries, write it once, test it once.
- A car that is itself held at a stop (waitT > 0, or speed < 1 with a sign)
  is not a conflict unless it has priority over me (below).

Priority rules, in order of simplicity:
- Two-way stop: signed approaches yield to everything unsigned. The unsigned
  through street never tests boxClear against signed cars at all.
- Left turn (signed or not): also yield to oncoming straight/right traffic.
  This is just one more row of the conflict table plus "they arrive first".
- Four-way stop: first-stopped goes first. Give each car a `stoppedAt = simT`
  when waitT starts; boxClear yields to any car at the same node with an
  earlier stoppedAt that is still waiting or crossing. Ties (within ~0.3s):
  yield to the car on your right -- their entry slot is one step clockwise
  from yours. This is both the real rule and a total order, so it cannot
  deadlock two cars; see deadlock note anyway.

Deadlock and livelock insurance
-------------------------------
Whatever the rules, add a per-car patience timer: if a car has been held at a
node for more than ~8-15s (personality again), it goes anyway. This is
realistic (people creep out eventually), it bounds every bug in the priority
logic to a temporary oddity instead of a frozen intersection, and it means the
conflict table can be wrong in a corner case without anyone ever noticing.
Cars still pass through each other if two decide to go at once -- keep that;
it is the existing, accepted failure mode, now rare instead of constant.

What NOT to build
-----------------
- No reservation/ticket system on the node, no per-node queues, no state that
  outlives a frame anywhere but on the car itself (stoppedAt, patience).
  Node-owned state has to be cleaned up when cars are deleted by the radius
  cull -- a whole class of stale-reference bugs the pure-read design cannot
  have. This is the main architectural instruction: boxClear reads, never
  writes.
- No path prediction beyond straight-line time-to-node. The windows plus
  margin absorb the error.
- No changes to the steering loop, the arcs, or following. If an intersection
  interaction seems to need steering changes, it doesn't; it needs a better
  hold decision.
- Don't make the player special. The player is already sensed geometrically in
  senseRoad; give boxClear the same treatment (a pseudo-approach built from
  the player's position/heading/speed toward the node) and traffic will yield
  to a player barreling through a four-way exactly as it should. Never assume
  the player follows any rule.

Performance
-----------
boxClear is O(cars at my node) and runs only for cars within ~100ft of a node,
plus every frame for cars holding at one. That is a handful of cars testing a
handful of cars; nothing here needs an index. If profiling ever disagrees,
bucket traffic by endNode once per frame in updateTraffic and hand each car
its bucket -- but measure first; the O(n^2) senseRoad pass at 135 cars costs
1.3ms and this is far smaller.

How to validate (use tools/render.js, see CLAUDE.md's Testing section)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Build the controlled two-street scenario from the turn tests (two or four
streets meeting at one hand-built node, hand-placed cars) and assert:
- A signed car waits while a cross car passes, then goes: order of node exit
  matches priority, no frame where both are inside the box.
- Four cars arriving at a four-way in a known order leave in that order.
- Two cars arriving simultaneously do not deadlock (patience or right-hand
  rule resolves it) -- run 60s, assert both eventually cross.
- A left-turner yields to an oncoming straight car but not to an oncoming
  right-turner leaving before the conflict point.
- City-wide regression: mean lane error, stop counts and honk rate from the
  measurements quoted in CLAUDE.md's Traffic section should not move much;
  throughput (cars crossing nodes per minute) should drop only modestly.
Watch a rendered PNG sequence of a busy four-way before trusting any numbers.

Implementation notes (what actually differed)
---------------------------------------------
1. The conflict table isn't a table. Numbering the arms 0-3 and putting each
   arm's entry lane at 2k and its exit lane at 2k+1 on a ring makes a movement
   the chord (2a, 2b+1), and two movements conflict iff their chords cross --
   eight lines that get every case right, including the ones the table would
   have had to enumerate, plus same-exit merges as a separate line. Cheaper to
   write and impossible to get subtly wrong in one row.
2. Two rules had to be added that are about *space*, not precedence, and both
   turned out to be load-bearing: never enter a box you have no room to leave
   (without it a queue extends through the junction and locks it solid -- this
   was the single biggest source of frozen intersections, and no priority rule
   can unpick it), and a vehicle longer than the box conflicts with everybody
   while it is in there, because a 40ft bus in a 24ft box is not a point on a
   lane path.
3. Crossing the line has to *commit* the driver. A car released into the box and
   re-blocked a moment later stops dead in the middle of it. Related: past the
   line, `-v^2/2d` has no finite answer, and without capping it the proportional
   loop only decays the last foot of speed asymptotically -- the car creeps into
   the junction for ever, which then trips every "already committed" heuristic.
4. Judge yourself and the other car with the *same* arrival formula. The launch
   model (real v^2 = u^2 + 2ad on the vehicle's own engine, which is what makes
   a cement truck wait for a gap a Countach ignores) is right for "how long will
   I take", but if the other fellow is measured at constant speed, both cars can
   conclude the other got there first and freeze. Antisymmetry is the property
   the whole priority scheme rests on; check it holds for every branch.
5. The stop line is where the *nose* stops, so the distance a car's centre keeps
   back is its own length. Otherwise a bus stops with its axle mid-junction and,
   worse, reads to everyone else as a vehicle standing in the box -- which under
   these rules is a thing everybody waits for.

One bug found while validating this that had nothing to do with right of way,
and was worth more than half the collision reduction: a block's traffic is
created inside pushStreet, a line before the new street is registered at its own
far node, and that node grows its remaining arms later still. A route picked
then can only be a U-turn -- a half circle across the whole box, arriving at
what is by then a four-way. Routes are now re-picked once on the way in
(REPLAN_DIST). Expect the same class of ordering trap anywhere traffic reads the
map at spawn time.


Plan: multi-lane roads, lane changing and merging
=================================================
(Same author, same ground rules. Nothing below exists yet -- streets today are
one lane each way and laneOffset() is a single number. Do the intersection work
above first; lane changes reuse its gap-acceptance ideas.)

Step 0: make "which lane" a first-class number
----------------------------------------------
Today a car's lateral intention is c.lane, a single offset in feet. Keep that
-- the steering loop should never learn about lanes -- but derive it:
`c.lane = laneCenter(street, dir, laneIndex)`, with c.laneIndex the driver's
intention (0 = rightmost). Street props grow `lanesPerSide` (1 for everything
current, so nothing changes until the generator starts making wider roads).
laneCenter is the one function that knows road geometry; everything else deals
in laneIndex. Grep for every reader of c.lane and laneOffset first; there are
only a handful (spawn, pose, planExit's A/B lane points, centerOff checks) and
each either wants laneCenter(...) or is already correct.

The lane change itself: it is only a moving target
--------------------------------------------------
Do not add a maneuver state machine to the steering loop. A lane change is
c.laneIndex changing by one, and c.lane easing toward the new centre --
literally `c.lane += (laneCenter(...) - c.lane) * min(1, dt / T)` with T ~
2-4s, or a constant-rate ramp. The PID follows the moving target and the
result already looks like a real lane change (the evade bias proved this
pattern: steering targets can move freely and the loop just tracks them).
Sharpen or soften per driver with T as a personality field. The only new
steering-adjacent code is the easing; keep it in driveCar's speed/target
section, not inside the PID.

Deciding to change: one function, same shape as boxClear
--------------------------------------------------------
`laneGapOk(c, dir)` -- a pure read over the same senseRoad iteration (extend
senseRoad to also report nearest-ahead and nearest-behind in the adjacent
lane; it already walks every car once). Accept when: gap ahead > my
minGap + headway*v against their speed, gap behind > their headway (estimate
with a fleet-typical constant -- do not read the other driver's brain, drivers
can't) with margin scaled by the same gapAccept personality from the
intersection work. Reasons to want a change, in priority order, evaluated
rarely (every ~0.5s per car, staggered, not every frame):
- Mandatory: my planned exit at the node needs a lane I am not in (see below).
  Wanted with growing urgency as the node nears; urgency relaxes gapAccept
  toward "force it slowly" (real drivers do), and below ~100ft failing that,
  fall back to slowing on the current lane and re-planning the exit to one my
  lane allows -- never stop dead in a live lane waiting for a gap.
- Discretionary: leader slower than my cruise by enough, adjacent lane's
  leader meaningfully faster, cooldown since my last change (personality:
  some drivers never change discretionarily, some weave).
Store almost nothing: laneIndex (intention), laneChangeT (cooldown/urgency
bookkeeping). No reservations, no signaling between cars beyond what senseRoad
already reads. Two cars converging on one gap resolves like merges today: the
later one sees a leader at negative gap and brakes; with laneGapOk it becomes
rare instead of routine.

Lanes at intersections
----------------------
planExit already decides the exit early; extend it to also pick the lane the
exit wants: rights from the rightmost lane (laneIndex 0), lefts from the
leftmost, straight from any. That decision is what feeds "mandatory" above,
and deciding it a whole block early means most cars drift over long before it
is urgent -- which is exactly the real behavior, and it costs nothing because
planExit already runs once per street. makeTurnPlan's A and B points become
laneCenter(street, dir, chosenLane) instead of the single lane offset; the
fillet geometry is unchanged. Turns should end in the matching lane of the new
street (rights into the right lane, lefts into the left) -- that is just which
B you hand the fillet -- and a discretionary change can move the car later.

Merging (lane count drops, on-ramps someday)
--------------------------------------------
A merge is a mandatory lane change whose deadline is where the lane ends, so
it is the same code path as "wrong lane for my exit": urgency ramps, gapAccept
relaxes, and the fallback is braking on the dying lane rather than stopping in
the through lane. If the generator ever tapers a road, mark the taper on the
street (fromPos, laneIndex dies) and mandatory-change logic reads it exactly
like a node deadline. Zipper politeness (the car in the through lane easing
off) is optional polish: reuse the following loop by letting a merger with
high urgency register as a half-weight leader for the car it is merging ahead
of -- one multiply on the gap error, only if it proves visually necessary.

Turn signals
------------
Purely visual, and the state already exists: signal = (laneChange in progress
or wanted) ? its direction : (turnPlan && near the node) ? turn direction : 0.
Compute it in driveCar (one field, c.signal = -1/0/1), never store history.
Draw as a small emissive quad pulsing on the appropriate body corner -- not
v.roof (that pass is for things above the whole vehicle, like the police light
bar) but the way flank trim/lights are done (v.trim, see vehicleUtils.js): a
2-3 poly pass gated on zoom like VEHICLE_GLASS_MIN_ZOOM, blinking on
(simT * 1.5) % 1 < 0.5. Drivers with low skill sometimes just don't signal
(personality bit); drunk weaving is not signaled -- only intended changes,
i.e. anything that moved laneIndex.

What NOT to build
-----------------
- No lateral planner, no spline paths for lane changes, no lattice of
  positions. The moving-target trick is the whole maneuver.
- No per-lane data structures on streets (occupancy grids, linked lists of
  cars per lane). senseRoad's single O(n) walk per car already sees everything
  and stays the one sensing pass; give it lane awareness by comparing
  laneIndex, nothing more.
- No cooperative protocols between cars. All coordination stays emergent:
  pure reads plus the following loop.
- Do not let the drunk's wander cross lanes "accidentally" trigger mandatory
  logic: wander perturbs where they think the centre is, laneIndex is still
  their intention. Off-centre is an error to correct, not a lane change.

Performance
-----------
All new decisions are per-street or per-half-second, staggered (hash car index
against frame count). The only per-frame additions are one easing lerp and two
extra comparisons inside the existing senseRoad loop. If lanesPerSide stays 1
the whole feature is dormant and costs two branches.

Validation
----------
- Build a hand-made 2-lane street (set lanesPerSide = 2 on props) with the
  harness; assert a car eases between lane centres in ~T seconds with lane
  error < 1ft throughout, no oscillation at the end.
- Slow leader in right lane, fast driver behind: passes on the left, returns
  or doesn't per personality; never clips the leader (world-distance check,
  like the merge-resolution test).
- Mandatory case: car in left lane planning a right turn 400ft out crosses
  over in time; with the gap blocked by a solid platoon, it slows and either
  finds the gap or re-plans the exit -- assert it never stops dead in the
  through lane and never turns from the wrong lane.
- Signals: probeCtx pass counting blink quads only during intended changes
  and near-node turns, never during drunk wander.
- Regression with lanesPerSide = 1 everywhere: pixel-identical frames (the
  dormant feature must not perturb today's cities -- same trick as the
  "suppress spawnStreetTraffic" determinism check in CLAUDE.md).
